GEX: Democratizing Dexterity with Fully-Actuated Dexterous Hand and Exoskeleton Glove

Code Docs

Abstract

This paper presents GEX, an open-source platform for accessible dexterous manipulation research that integrates a fully actuated robotic hand, a matching wearable interface, and an end-to-end software stack for teleoperation and robot learning. GEX combines GX16, a four-finger anthropomorphic hand with 16 independently actuated degrees of freedom, and EX16, a 16-DoF exoskeleton glove for finger-motion capture and contact-triggered force feedback. Both devices use modular joint units and primarily 3D-printed components, with an estimated parts cost of approximately $600 per device, or $1,200 for the complete hand-glove system. To bridge the different kinematic structures of the glove and the robotic hand, we formulate retargeting in a shared morphology-aligned workspace and train a neural inverse-kinematics model through a differentiable inverse- and forward-kinematics loop. In addition to fingertip-position reconstruction and sparse joint anchors, the proposed method introduces a Jacobian-Cycle regularizer that promotes local consistency in displacement direction, gain, and cross-finger motion. The released software covers communication, low-level control, motion retargeting, data collection, and deployment on physical hardware. We evaluate GEX through real-world manipulation demonstrations, contact-rich teleoperation tasks, imitation learning from human demonstrations, and simulation-to-real reinforcement learning. The experiments demonstrate coordinated multi-finger grasping, in-hand reorientation, twisting, insertion, and stacked-object grasping, establishing GEX as an integrated and reproducible testbed for dexterous teleoperation and robot skill learning.
